An experiment shows how each variant of a feature converts against a control, with uncertainty and risk alongside the headline rate. You connect an experiment to a [multivariate feature flag](/feature-flag-analytics) and a [goal](/conversion-goal-tracking); TinyAnalytics counts conversions, compares posterior evidence, and shows progress over time without selecting a winner for you.

## Before you start

- A **multivariate feature flag** with the variants you want to test (for example `control` and `treatment`).
- A conversion you want to measure — a signup, a purchase, or a key page view. You can select an existing goal, create one in the experiment wizard, or add one later.
- Your app calls `window.tinyanalytics.flag()` where the visitor experiences the variant. This is what makes the measurement accurate.

## How do I choose the experiment goal?

In the experiment wizard, choose **Create a new goal**, **Use existing**, or **Skip for now**. A new goal can match a page path or a custom event. For an event goal, start typing to filter event names recorded in the dashboard's current date range; you can still enter a new name that has no data in that range.

Skipping lets you create the experiment and attach its primary goal later. Conversion, lift, and significance results need a goal before TinyAnalytics can calculate them.

## How does an experiment count conversions?

The unit of measurement is the **session**, not the pageview. For each variant, TinyAnalytics counts the sessions that saw it and the sessions that then completed your goal. Attribution is **post-exposure**: a conversion only counts if it happened *at or after* the visitor first saw the variant.

- A session that converts *before* ever seeing the variant is **not** credited to it — a variant can't take credit for behavior that preceded it.
- A session that converts twice counts **once**.
- Conversions can never exceed what your goal reports for the same period, because the experiment reuses the goal's own definition.

## Where should I call flag()?

Call `window.tinyanalytics.flag()` at the moment the visitor experiences the variant. This is the one step that decides whether your results are trustworthy. The call records an **exposure** — proof that this session actually experienced the variant — and the experiment counts those exposed sessions as its primary, statistically correct measurement.

```js
// Fire the exposure at the moment the visitor experiences the variant
const variant = window.tinyanalytics.flag('checkout-test', 'control')
render(variant === 'treatment' ? <NewCheckout /> : <OldCheckout />)
```

<Warning>
  If no session ever calls `flag()` for the experiment's flag, TinyAnalytics falls back to an **assignment** measurement — everyone the flag *would* have bucketed — and flags the results as less reliable, because assignment doesn't prove anyone saw the difference. Call `flag()` at the point of experience to keep your experiment on the accurate measurement.
</Warning>

## How do I read the results?

Each variant shows its **sessions**, **conversions**, **conversion rate**, and **lift** versus the control:

- **Control** is the variant named `control`, or the first one you declared. Every variant you declared appears in the table even before it has any traffic.
- **Lift** is the variant's conversion rate relative to control. It reads as blank (not "0%") when the control itself hasn't converted yet — there's no baseline to compare against.
- **Statistical assessment** uses a beta-posterior comparison against control, rather than a simple z-test. TinyAnalytics needs at least **100 sessions per active arm** and **10 total conversions** before showing comparative evidence. Until then it shows **"Gathering data."**

TinyAnalytics never auto-picks a winner and applies no multiple-comparison correction. Statistical assessments and projections inform your decision; the call to ship stays yours.

## How do I read uncertainty and progress?

When enough data exists, each non-control variant shows:

- **Chance to beat control** — the posterior probability that its conversion rate is higher. Values near 0% or 100% remain bounded instead of appearing certain.
- **95% lift interval** — a relative-lift range, so a noisy percentage does not look like a precise outcome.
- **Expected loss if chosen** — the estimated conversion-rate cost of choosing that variant incorrectly. This is a risk measure, not a promised loss.
- **Projected scale** — when a comparison is not yet clear, an estimate of how much more session volume could produce a decision if current rates hold. It is a projection, not a date or guarantee.

A **sample-ratio mismatch** warning appears when observed exposure counts disagree with the configured arm weights at `p < 0.001`. Check allocation before trusting those comparisons. Assignment-fallback results keep their measurement caveat and do not pretend assignment is verified exposure, so they do not run this check.

The **cumulative conversion rate** chart uses the same date window, filters, post-exposure attribution, and exposure/assignment mode as the summary. It zero-fills missing days and shows at most 366 days. When a longer window is truncated, earlier totals carry into the first visible point so the chart still agrees with the summary.

## Frequently asked questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Why does my experiment say 'Gathering data'?">
    Each active arm needs at least **100 sessions**, with **10 total conversions**, before TinyAnalytics shows comparative evidence. Until then it shows "Gathering data" rather than claiming statistical confidence.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why is the lift column blank for my variant?">
    The control hasn't converted yet, so there's no baseline to compare against. TinyAnalytics shows lift as blank rather than "0%" in that case. Lift is the variant's conversion rate relative to control.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Do conversions that happen before a visitor sees the variant count?">
    No. Attribution in TinyAnalytics experiments is **post-exposure** — a conversion only counts if it happened at or after the visitor first saw the variant. A session that converts twice still counts once.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does TinyAnalytics pick the winning variant for me?">
    No. TinyAnalytics never auto-picks a winner and applies no multiple-comparison correction. It surfaces which variant is leading once that lead is statistically significant, and the decision to ship stays yours.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
