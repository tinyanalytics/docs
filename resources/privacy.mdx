tinyanalytics is built to be **privacy-friendly by design**. It sets no cookies, stores no raw IP addresses, and identifies visitors with a one-way hash instead of a persistent tracker — so by default, no personal data is stored and nothing is placed on your visitor's device.

## The privacy properties

- **No cookies.** tinyanalytics never writes a cookie or other identifier to the visitor's browser.
- **No raw IP addresses stored.** A visitor's IP is used only in memory to derive a [hashed identifier](/resources/cookieless-identity) and an approximate location, then it's gone — it isn't kept with your data.
- **No cross-site profile.** The identifier is a hash, not a stored ID that follows a person across the web.
- **No personal data by default.** Names, emails, and other identifying details are stored only if *you* deliberately send them via `identify()`, traits, or event properties.

## Do you still need a cookie-consent banner?

Because tinyanalytics sets no cookies and stores no personal data by default, many sites can run it **without a cookie-consent banner**. Whether *you* need consent still depends on your jurisdiction and on what data you choose to send — so review the rules that apply to you and your visitors.

<Note>
  This page describes how tinyanalytics works, not legal advice. Your obligations under GDPR, ePrivacy, CCPA, or other regulations depend on your specific use, the data you collect, and where your visitors are. Consult those regulations — or a professional — for your situation.
</Note>

## Keeping data collection privacy-friendly

You decide how much personal data, if any, enters tinyanalytics:

- Use a **non-identifying ID** (like an internal user ID or a hash) with [`identify()`](/guides/identify-users) rather than an email address.
- Keep raw personal information out of [custom event](/guides/custom-events) properties and traits unless you have a reason and a basis to store it.
- Review what your team sends periodically, since properties and traits are free-form.
