tinyanalytics is a **hosted analytics service**: your site sends events, and tinyanalytics stores and reports on them for you. This page explains what's stored, what's never stored, and how your data stays yours.

## What tinyanalytics stores

For each event your site sends, tinyanalytics keeps the analytics context it needs to build your reports:

- **Page context** — the path, hostname, referrer, page title, and any campaign (UTM) parameters.
- **Derived attributes** — an approximate location (country, region, city) from the IP, the marketing [channel](/resources/traffic-classification), and the device, browser, and OS from the user agent.
- **A cookieless identifier** — a one-way [hashed fingerprint](/resources/cookieless-identity), not a stored ID.
- **What you choose to send** — [custom events](/guides/custom-events) and their properties, plus any user ID and [traits](/guides/identify-users) you set with `identify()`.

## What tinyanalytics never stores

- **No cookies.** Nothing is written to your visitor's device.
- **No raw IP addresses.** A visitor's IP is used only, in memory, to derive the hashed identifier and approximate location — it's never written to your analytics data.
- **No personal data by default.** tinyanalytics stores no names, emails, or other identifying details unless *you* deliberately send them as an identified ID, a trait, or an event property.

<Warning>
  Because custom event properties and traits are free-form, you control whether any personal data enters tinyanalytics. Avoid putting raw personal information (like an email address) into event properties unless you intend to store it.
</Warning>

## Your data stays isolated and yours

Each site's data is separate, and access is gated by [organization membership and teams](/guides/teams) — one organization can never read another's analytics. You can [export](/guides/export) your data as CSV or PDF, or query it directly with the [SQL query builder](/guides/sql-query-builder) and the [read API](/api-reference/read), at any time.

Retention is applied at **read time**: a retention limit bounds how far back you can query, rather than deleting your event data.
