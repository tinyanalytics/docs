The Pages report ranks your site's pages by how many people visit them. Each row is a page, with its number of visitors, its pageviews, and a small trend sparkline, so you can see your top content at a glance and how each page is trending.

## What each column means

| Column | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Page** | The page's title and its path, with a link to open the live page. |
| **Trend** | A sparkline of the page's visits over your selected date range. |
| **Sessions** | The number of distinct sessions that viewed the page. |
| **Pageviews** | The total number of times the page was viewed. |

Pages are ranked by visitors, most-visited first.

## How do I switch to titles, entries, exits, or hostnames?

The Pages section has tabs that re-slice the same traffic by a different key, so you can answer different questions from one place:

| View | What it ranks |
| --- | --- |
| **Pages** | Pages by path — your top content. |
| **Titles** | Pages by their `<title>` — useful when one path serves many titles. |
| **Entries** | **Entry pages** — the first page of each session, ranked by how many sessions start there. |
| **Exits** | **Exit pages** — the last page of each session, ranked by how many sessions end there. |
| **Hostnames** | Traffic by hostname — useful when one site tracks several domains or subdomains. |

## How do I read the Landing pages report?

A **landing page** is the first page a visitor sees in a session — where the visit begins. The **Landing pages** report ranks your entry pages and adds the engagement metrics that tell you whether each one actually holds attention:

| Column | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Landing page** | The path where sessions started, with a link to open it. |
| **Trend** | A sparkline of sessions starting on this page over your date range. |
| **Sessions** | Sessions that began here, with the change versus the previous period. |
| **Bounce** | The share of those sessions that viewed only this one page and left. |
| **Avg. duration** | How long those sessions lasted on average. |
| **Avg. scroll** | How far down the landing page visitors scrolled on average. |
| **Avg. time** | The average time spent on the landing page itself. |

<Accordion title="How bounce and missing engagement values are calculated">
  Bounce here is measured against each session's **real, unfiltered pageview count** — so applying a dashboard filter can't inflate or deflate it. Missing engagement data shows as "—" rather than zero, so a page with no scroll signal isn't mistaken for one nobody scrolled.
</Accordion>

<Tip>
  Read landing pages to judge your **acquisition quality**, not just volume: a page that draws many sessions but bounces most of them is pulling the wrong traffic — or making the wrong first impression.
</Tip>

## How do I filter the dashboard by one page?

**Click a row to filter the whole dashboard by that page.** Every metric, chart, and breakdown then re-scopes to visitors who saw it — a quick way to answer "how does this one page perform?" Click the row again to clear the filter. See [filter your data](/guides/filters).

## Common workflows

- **Find your top content.** The default ranking shows which pages drive the most traffic.
- **Check a page's momentum.** Read the trend sparkline to see whether a page is gaining or losing visits.
- **Drill into one page.** Click a page to filter the dashboard, then read its sources, devices, and geography in the [Overview](/guides/dashboard-overview) breakdowns.

## Frequently asked questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="How are pages ranked in the Pages report?">
    Pages are ranked by visitors, most-visited first. Each row shows the page's title and path, a trend sparkline over your selected date range, the number of distinct sessions that viewed it, and its total pageviews.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What's the difference between the Pages and Titles views?">
    The Pages view ranks pages by their path, which is the standard read of your top content. The Titles view ranks them by their HTML `<title>` instead, which is useful when one path serves many titles. Both views re-slice the same traffic by a different key.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What's the difference between an entry page and an exit page?">
    An entry page is the first page of a session and an exit page is the last. In the tinyanalytics Pages report, the **Entries** view ranks pages by how many sessions start there, and the **Exits** view ranks them by how many sessions end there.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How do I see stats for a single page?">
    Click that page's row in the Pages report. Every metric, chart, and breakdown on the dashboard re-scopes to visitors who saw it, so you can read its sources, devices, and geography. Click the row again to clear the filter. See [filter your data](/guides/filters).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
