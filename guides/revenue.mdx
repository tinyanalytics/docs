Revenue analytics ties money to your traffic. You attach a **`revenue`** amount and a **`currency`** to any [custom event](/guides/custom-events) — a purchase, an upgrade, a renewal — and tinyanalytics rolls it up into total revenue, average order value, and revenue by channel, country, and device, so you can see which traffic actually pays.

## Send revenue with an event

Add `revenue` and `currency` to any custom event you already send. These are **reserved property names** — tinyanalytics recognizes them and treats the event as a sale:

```js
window.tinyanalytics.event('purchase', {
  revenue: 49.99,
  currency: 'USD'
})
```

The rules are simple:

- **`revenue`** is the amount in **major units** — dollars, euros, pounds — not cents. `49.99` means $49.99, not 49 cents. It accepts a number or a numeric string, and must be positive.
- **`currency`** is a **3-letter code** like `USD`, `EUR`, or `GBP` (case-insensitive).
- Only **custom events** carry revenue. A pageview never does.

<Note>
  Send one revenue event per completed sale, at the moment it completes — for a web store, on the order-confirmation page. Refunds and negative amounts aren't supported; revenue only adds up.
</Note>

You can send the same event [from your server](/api-reference/track) with an [API key](/api-reference/api-keys) — useful when the true "paid" moment is a webhook from your payment processor rather than a page in the browser.

## Read the Revenue report

The Revenue report leads with four headline metrics, each with its change versus the previous period:

| Metric | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Total revenue** | The sum of all revenue in the period, in your reporting currency. |
| **Avg. order value** | Total revenue divided by the number of orders. |
| **Paying sessions** | Distinct sessions that produced at least one revenue event. |
| **Orders** | The number of revenue-bearing events. |

Below that, a **revenue-over-time chart** shows the trend across your date range, and a **"Revenue by" table** breaks revenue down across dimensions — channel, country, region, referrer, AI assistant, device, browser, operating system, and UTM tags.

<Tip>
  Revenue in the breakdown is credited to the **session's entry acquisition** — the source that first brought the visitor in. That means revenue-by-channel reconciles with sessions-by-channel: the channel that *won the visit* gets the credit for the sale, even if the purchase happened pages later.
</Tip>

## Reporting currency

You can receive revenue in many currencies but read your reports in one. Set a **reporting currency** in your site's [general settings](/guides/site-settings) (the default is USD). Every amount is **stored in the currency it was sent in**, then converted to your reporting currency when a report is read.

Conversion uses built-in exchange rates for **22 major currencies** (USD, EUR, GBP, JPY, and the like), refreshed with each release — they're approximate, not a live foreign-exchange feed. A valid currency code that isn't in that set is stored and counted at a 1:1 rate rather than dropped, so no revenue ever goes missing. For exact multi-currency accounting, treat the reporting-currency total as a close estimate.

## Revenue everywhere else

Once your events carry revenue, it powers more than the Revenue report:

- **[Goals](/guides/goals)** — a revenue goal sums the money behind a conversion, not just its count.
- **[Funnels](/guides/funnels)** — each step shows the revenue of the sessions that reached it.
- **[Experiments](/guides/experiments)** — compare revenue and revenue-per-visitor across variants, counting only purchases after a visitor was exposed.
- **[Alerts](/guides/alerts)** — get notified when revenue crosses a threshold you set.
