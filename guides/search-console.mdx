Connect **Google Search Console** to tinyanalytics to see how people find you in Google Search — your top queries, pages, countries, and devices, each with clicks, impressions, click-through rate, and average position — right alongside your traffic analytics. It answers the question your pageview data can't: *what did people search to get here?*

## How do I connect Google Search Console?

You connect Search Console from its report page in three steps. You'll need a Google account that already has verified Search Console properties.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Search Console and connect">
    Go to the **Search Console** report for your site and click **Connect**. You'll be sent to Google to sign in and grant read access to your Search Console data.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick a property">
    After you approve, tinyanalytics lists the Search Console properties your Google account can access. Choose the one that matches this site.
  </Step>
  <Step title="View your search metrics">
    Your queries, pages, countries, and devices load in the report. Everything stays on this one page — connect, view, and disconnect are all here.
  </Step>
</Steps>

tinyanalytics only ever **reads** your Search Console data. You can disconnect at any time from the same page.

## What search metrics does tinyanalytics show?

The report breaks your Search Console data down across four dimensions, one tab each, and every row carries the same four metrics:

| Dimension | Metrics per row |
| --- | --- |
| **Queries** — what people searched | Clicks · Impressions · CTR · Average position |
| **Pages** — which of your pages appeared | Clicks · Impressions · CTR · Average position |
| **Countries** — where searchers were | Clicks · Impressions · CTR · Average position |
| **Devices** — desktop, mobile, tablet | Clicks · Impressions · CTR · Average position |

Click a column header to sort. CTR is shown as a percentage, and average position to one decimal place.

## Why is Search Console on its own date range?

Search Console data is **daily and lags about two to three days** behind, so the report uses its own trailing window — **7, 28, or 90 days** — rather than the minute-level range the rest of the dashboard shares. A "last 30 minutes" view would be meaningless for search data that hasn't arrived yet.

## What is the Search Console card on my dashboard?

Once a site is connected, your main dashboard shows a **Search Console** card with your top five queries over the last 28 days — a quick glance at what's driving search traffic without leaving the overview. The card appears only when Search Console is connected, so an unconnected dashboard stays uncluttered.

<Info>
  Search Console queries can be more sensitive than aggregate pageviews, so the report and its dashboard card are visible only to signed-in members of your site — never on a public or embedded dashboard.
</Info>

## Frequently asked questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Why is my Search Console data two or three days behind?">
    Google Search Console data is daily and lags about two to three days behind. That's why the tinyanalytics Search Console report uses its own trailing window — 7, 28, or 90 days — instead of the minute-level date range the rest of the dashboard shares.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can I disconnect Google Search Console from tinyanalytics?">
    Yes, at any time from the Search Console report page — connect, view, and disconnect all live on that one page. tinyanalytics only ever reads your Search Console data; it never writes to it.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does Search Console data appear on a public or embedded dashboard?">
    No. Search Console queries can be more sensitive than aggregate pageviews, so the report and its dashboard card are visible only to signed-in members of your site. A public or embedded tinyanalytics dashboard never shows them.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
